## Method 03: Problem definition and how-might-we questions

Phase 3 of Method 3. Frames validated themes as actionable direction without prescribing solutions.

> **Revision note (2026-08-01):** An earlier version scoped this problem to accessibility of data-dense surfaces. That was a narrowing error — one vivid finding from a single examined codebase was allowed to stand in for the whole problem space. This revision restores the intended scope: agentic support for UI/UX practice across the full asset set, grounded in open and community standards. Accessibility is one concern within that scope, not its definition.

## Who this is for

UI/UX designers and the teams they work with. Where a project has no dedicated designer, the same practice is carried by engineers, product managers, or a tech lead. The capability serves the **UX practitioner role**, whoever currently holds it.

## Problem definition

> UI/UX practitioners are expected to produce a substantial and varied body of work — outcomes, personas, jobs analyses, journeys, trade studies, requirements, test plans, design systems, handoff specifications, and measurement definitions. Most of it is authored by hand, in inconsistent formats, disconnected from the engineering process that consumes it, and grounded in whatever methods the individual practitioner happens to know.
>
> The available agentic support covers a narrow slice of that body of work, speaks a vocabulary the surrounding engineering process does not use, and cites no methodological grounding a practitioner could point to and defend.

Supporting themes: T2, T5, T6.

## Secondary problem definitions

> **Standards grounding is absent, not merely thin.** Established, well-documented methods exist for most of what UX practitioners do — Jobs-to-be-Done for demand-side framing, journey and service mapping for experience structure, published usability characteristics for quality definition, open design-token formats for handoff, authoring-tool guidance for tool conformance. Current agentic support cites none of them. A practitioner cannot tell what an output is grounded in, and cannot defend it to a stakeholder who asks.

Supporting themes: T4, T6.

> **The practice is fragmented rather than unsupported.** Capabilities for scanning, surface inventory, screen capture, review perspectives, and planning workflows already exist independently. They share no vocabulary, no linkage, and no common artifact, so a practitioner encounters five disconnected tools rather than one coherent practice.

Supporting theme: T5.

> **Design intent and verification do not reference each other.** Where verification exists, it evaluates built software against generic rules. Nothing feeds results back into design authoring, and nothing feeds design intent forward into what gets verified. A design decision that was never made is invisible to every tool in the chain.

Supporting themes: T1, T3.

## How-Might-We questions

### Primary

**HMW1.** How might we support the full range of assets a UX practitioner is expected to produce, rather than an arbitrary subset?
*From T2, T6.*

**HMW2.** How might we ground each asset in a named, open method a practitioner can cite and defend?
*From T4, T6.*

**HMW3.** How might we support the practitioner's actual workflow — iterative, non-linear, interleaved with engineering work — rather than a linear artifact pipeline?
*From T6.*

**HMW4.** How might we connect capabilities that already exist so that a practitioner encounters one coherent practice rather than five disconnected tools?
*From T5.*

### Secondary

**HMW5.** How might we speak the vocabulary the surrounding engineering process already uses, so that output has an obvious place to go?
*From T6. Strength: Medium — vocabulary mismatch verified, adoption inference not.*

**HMW6.** How might we let design intent and verification reference the same thing, so that an unmade decision is as visible as a failed check?
*From T3.*

**HMW7.** How might we make quality attributes that are normally deferred — accessibility, usability, inclusivity — decisions a practitioner makes rather than omissions they discover later?
*From T1, T2.*

**HMW8.** How might we offer the right fidelity at the right moment, so that rigor arrives when a real system exists and questions arrive when it does not?
*From T7. Strength: Medium.*

### Constraint carried forward

**HMW9.** How might we validate any of this against real UX practitioners before committing to it?
*From T8. Not a design question — a research obligation carried into every subsequent method.*

## Scope correction record

The narrowing that produced the earlier revision is itself evidence worth keeping. It illustrates a failure mode this capability should be designed against: **a single vivid finding displacing a broader practice.** A tool that surfaces one quality attribute forcefully will distort a practitioner's attention the same way it distorted this session's.

Specific correction: JTBD and journey mapping were previously recorded as low-evidence candidates for cutting. They are established community methods and belong in the asset set. Their demotion was an artifact of the narrowed frame, not a finding.

## What these questions deliberately do not prescribe

* Not whether the capability is a skill, an agent, a subagent, an instruction file, or several.
* Not which specific assets make the first cut.
* Not which standards get vendored into reference material.
* Not whether Design Thinking methods are modified.
